# The Gift of the Magi — independent screening brief

Draft for a reviewer chosen or approved by Dan Cockrell. Not sent to anyone.

An AI-assisted narrated adaptation of O. Henry's story, approximately fifteen
minutes. This is an editorial review copy, not the final festival submission.
Please watch once with sound at normal speed before consulting production notes.
We want your judgment of the film, not reassurance about the technology.

## First viewing

1. At what moment, if any, did you begin to care about Della and Jim?
2. Where did your attention drift? Please give approximate times and what you
   felt was missing: action, change, clarity, tension, or emotional connection.
3. Was any physical action or story development confusing? Describe what you
   thought happened before looking for an explanation.
4. Did the final exchange move you? What in the filmmaking helped or prevented it?
5. Which performance or image would you protect in another edit?

## Second look, if you have time

- Does each cut preserve the action and emotional state, or does a person appear
  to reset, reverse course or repeat a gesture?
- Do props, costume, hair, reflections and room geography support the story?
- Does the title opening invite you in at the right pace?
- Can you understand every line comfortably? Do music and effects deepen the
  scene, compete with it, or call attention to a technical boundary?
- Does the film end cleanly, with enough space for the emotion to land?

Please separate a visible observation from your explanation of its cause.
“Her hand appears twice” is evidence; a theory about the generator is not needed.
Disagreement with the director's intention is useful: tell us what the scene
actually communicates to you.

## Editorial response record

Record reviewer name/role with their permission, date, exact movie version,
listening setup, and timestamps. Keep their words distinct from our interpretation.
For each actionable note, record preserve / recut / reshoot / sound change /
decline with reason. Do not translate a positive response into an award prediction.

Before an entry is approved, seek at least one experienced editorial assessment
and fresh-viewer feedback. The film's creator and production agent are not an
independent panel. Obtain permission before sharing the movie or quoting feedback.
